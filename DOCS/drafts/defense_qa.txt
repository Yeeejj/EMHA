1. How do you know there is no leakage?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Splitting is by participant, never by crop. DATASET/metadata/folds.csv holds
one row per participant (src/training/splits.py, make_outer_folds), so all 24
crops of a person sit in one fold. Every runner calls
splits.assert_no_leakage on train, test, and middle-band IDs in every fold
(run_baselines.py, run_cnn.py, permutation.py; CrossValidator in
cross_validate.py also checks inner train and validation). run_hybrid._check_split
refuses a CNN checkpoint whose stored split differs. Scalers and PCA sit inside
sklearn Pipelines fitted on training participants only (run_baselines.fit_lr).
The evaluator rejects any participant predicted twice (evaluator.validate).
tests/test_splits.py tests these guards, e.g.
test_one_row_per_participant_no_participant_in_two_folds.

3. Why an HMM on offline images?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Offline scans lose pen timing, but writing still runs left to right. The
fine-tuned CNN's layer3 map, averaged over height, gives one feature vector
per image column; read left to right, these form a sequence per word or
cursive crop (CNNConfig.seq_layer; src/models/hybrid.py, crop_sequences). One
Gaussian HMM per class models how those column features change along the
line (src/models/hmm.py). Topology, state count, and PCA size are chosen on
inner-validation participants (HybridConfig; run_hybrid.select_hmm). Whether
the sequence model adds anything is tested, not assumed: the backbone
ablation compares the CNN head with the CNN-HMM (secondary.txt > 3.
Ablations) [PENDING]. Limitation: the HMMs see features of images the CNN
trained on (run_hybrid.py docstring; protocol section 5).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. Are the self-report labels reliable?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The FINALE instrument was designed with 4 registered psychometricians
(CLAUDE.md; DOCS/FINALE Thesis Assessment.pdf). It has 24 Likert items, 12
reverse-scored (config.py, ReportConfig). Labels are read verbatim from the
scoring export and never recomputed (src/data/labeler.py). Internal
consistency is Cronbach's alpha with a bootstrap CI
(src/analysis/questionnaire_report.py) [PENDING: alpha value;
results/questionnaire/report.txt not yet generated]. The extreme-groups
design sets aside participants nearest the cutoff, where a label is least
certain (DOCS/decisions/analysis_design.txt). The labels remain concurrent
self-report, not diagnosis, and that is the stated scope
(DOCS/drafts/methods.txt, section 1).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. Why no binarization or deskew?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The proposal planned both (README.md, Methodology). They were dropped before
any model was run because they delete candidate signal. Binarization removes
ink darkness, so stroke-intensity variation cannot be measured. Deskewing
removes baseline slope and slant, which are measured as features
(baseline_angle_deg, slant_deg in src/features/handcrafted.py). Stretch
resizing would distort size and aspect ratio. The pipeline does grayscale
background flattening, one fixed scale per family, and white padding only
(src/preprocessing/pipeline.py docstring; PreprocessingConfig). Otsu appears
only in descriptive statistics, never in a model input. Augmentation
excludes rotation, scaling, shear, and flips for the same reason
(AugmentConfig).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. How does this compare with online-tablet studies such as EMOTHAW?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
EMOTHAW recorded 129 participants on a digitizing tablet, with pen position,
in-air movement, timing, and pressure, across seven tasks including house
drawing and cursive copying. Labels were DASS anxiety, depression, and
stress. Random forests reached 60% to 71% (CITATIONS/EMOTHAW_A_novel_database_
for_emotional_state_recog.pdf, abstract). This study uses offline 200 dpi
scans (IngestConfig) with no timing, in-air, or pressure channel, and a
binary HAPPY/SAD self-report. The numbers are therefore not directly
comparable: different labels, inputs, and samples. Our result [PENDING:
RESULTS.txt > PRIMARY RESULTS] shows what is recoverable from paper alone,
which matches how handwriting is usually collected.

7. Is it better than chance?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Two checks, both fixed in advance (EVALUATION_PROTOCOL.md section 4).
First, every accuracy is shown beside the majority baseline: the training-fold
majority label (src/training/evaluator.py, majority_hits). Second, a
permutation test shuffles training labels within each outer fold, refits the
whole pipeline, and scores test participants on their true labels;
p = (1 + #null >= observed) / (1 + N) (src/analysis/permutation.py). N is
1,000 for the two ensemble logistic regressions and 20 full retrains for
the CNN models and the ensemble (PermutationConfig; protocol section 4,
Revision 3; smallest attainable p = 1/21 = 0.0476). The test is checked on
synthetic data: not significant without signal, and the smallest attainable
p with signal (tests/test_permutation.py). Real values: [PENDING: RESULTS.txt
> PRIMARY RESULTS, permutation column].

8. Why set aside the middle band, and how is that not cherry-picking?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Participants near the cutoff carry the least certain labels. The band
fraction (0.20) was chosen on 2026-10-04 from labels.csv scores only, before
any model existed, as the smallest previewed fraction that kept at least 100
per class (DOCS/decisions/analysis_design.txt; commit 2bb867a). Membership
never depends on predictions (EVALUATION_PROTOCOL.md section 1). The protocol
was approved on 2026-10-05 and is frozen by the git tag protocol-frozen;
runners refuse real data until the tag exists
(src/utils/protocol_guard.py). The full sample is reported alongside, and the
middle band is predicted as a secondary analysis (protocol section 6).

11. Why is the CNN-HMM primary if the protocol named the ensemble?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The CNN-HMM is the proposed method: it is named in the thesis title and in
the approved proposal, and the ensemble was added by the protocol as a
combined reference. The change is Deviation 4 (EVALUATION_PROTOCOL.md
section 9). It was made after the full run's results were read, it is
disclosed there and in the paper, and nothing was recomputed: same models,
folds, band, seeds, threshold, and metrics. It does not favour the
headline: on the primary set the CNN-HMM's macro-F1 is 0.434 against the
ensemble's 0.516 (RESULTS.txt > PRIMARY RESULTS). The ensemble is still
reported in full as the supporting model, with its CI, paired comparison,
and permutation p.

12. Why is the CNN-HMM's accuracy 53.4% but balanced accuracy 50.1%?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
It predicted HAPPY for 281 of the 320 primary-set respondents: 153 of 174
HAPPY and 18 of 146 SAD were correct. Its participant P(SAD) ranged only
from 0.34 to 0.58, and its ROC-AUC was 0.485. Accuracy therefore sits near
the majority rate (0.544), which comes from the larger HAPPY class;
balanced accuracy, the mean of the two per-class recalls, is near 0.5.
Source: results/hybrid/primary/predictions.csv, cnn_hmm_fused/fused rows
with in_middle_band == False; RESULTS.txt > PRIMARY RESULTS.

13. What is CNN-HMM v2 and what did it show?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
v2 is an exploratory, post hoc model (EVALUATION_PROTOCOL.md section 9,
Deviation 5). Each respondent is one sequence of 24 frozen ImageNet
ResNet18 embeddings, one per task image, in FINALE form order (D1-D4,
W1_LH ... W5_UC, CS1-CS5); one Gaussian HMM per class, selected on inner
validation exactly as v1, Platt-calibrated, threshold 0.5
(src/training/run_hybrid_v2.py). On the primary set (n = 320) it reached
macro-F1 0.480 [0.427, 0.536], accuracy 0.537 [0.484, 0.594], balanced
accuracy 0.512 [0.468, 0.558], ROC-AUC 0.487 [0.423, 0.550], against a
majority baseline of 0.544; the permutation test gave p = 0.5842
(N = 100, full refit per draw). It identified 33 of 146 SAD respondents.
Source: results/final/EXPLORATORY_v2.txt.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
14. Why isn't v2 the main result if it scored higher than v1?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
v2 was designed after all results were seen, so it cannot be a
confirmatory test. Deviation 5 fixed its whole design (features, order,
grid, selection rule, N = 100 permutations) and was committed before v2
ran on real data; nothing was changed after its result. Its gain over v1
is not distinguishable from zero: paired macro-F1 difference v2 - v1
+0.047 [-0.024, +0.115] (EXPLORATORY_v2.txt section 4). Its permutation
p = 0.5842 is also not below 0.05. v1 remains the primary model
(Deviation 4); v2 is reported in full as exploratory.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
15. Why did the HMM fail even with a real order?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The settings it chose looked useful on inner validation but did not carry
over: the selected setting's inner-validation macro-F1 ranged from 0.538
to 0.675 across folds, while the pooled test macro-F1 was 0.480 (per-fold
0.408 to 0.513). With 39 inner-validation participants per fold
(results/hybrid_v2/primary/selection.json) the selection is noisy and
optimistic. The features themselves were not
the problem: the same frozen embeddings averaged per participant
(lr_embeddings/concat) reached macro-F1 0.576, p = 0.0080 (RESULTS.txt),
and v2 was below them on the same resamples, -0.094 [-0.163, -0.024]
(EXPLORATORY_v2.txt section 4). Modelling transitions between 24 very
different task images adds parameters without adding signal at n = 320.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
16. Why train per exercise?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
My adviser asked that each exercise be trained and evaluated one by one, to
see which of the 24 FINALE tasks carry information about the self-reported
state. The family models (v1) pool the crops of a task family, so they
cannot say whether a signal comes from, say, the house-and-tree drawing or the
melancholic word. v3 answers that question as an exploratory analysis
(EVALUATION_PROTOCOL.md section 9, Deviation 6). It was added after the v1
and v2 results were known, so it is not part of the primary claim; v1
stays the primary model. Same participants, folds, labels, inner split,
seed and 0.5 threshold as v1 (DOCS/drafts/methods.txt, section 12).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
17. Isn't picking the best exercise cherry-picking?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
It would be, if I took the highest row of the 24-exercise table and called
it the result. That is selection on test participants, and the v3 plan
rules it out (DOCS/plans/EMHA_V3_MASTER_PLAN.txt, leakage rule L9). The only
"best exercise" model I report is cnn_hmm_v3_selected: in each outer fold it
picks the exercise with the highest inner-validation macro-F1, using only
that fold's 39 inner-validation participants, and then scores the fold's
test participants. It reached macro-F1 0.487 [0.431, 0.542] and ROC-AUC
0.469 [0.404, 0.530], and it picked a different exercise in every fold (CS1,
W2_RH, W1_UC, CS4, W3_UC). The per-exercise rows are reported as
descriptive, all 96 of them, and the LR p-values are corrected with
Benjamini-Hochberg within each model; no test reached q <= 0.05.
Source: DOCS/plans/v3_result.txt.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
18. Why only one CNN-path test, and why not more draws?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
One test, because 24 exercises x 2 CNN models is 48 chances to find
something by luck. So the CNN path got a single test declared before any
v3 result: cnn_hmm_v3_fused/all24. Each draw retrains all 24 exercises in
all 5 folds, about 61 to 67 minutes on one NVIDIA L4. The number of draws
was set by a rule written before training (Deviation 6, D2): 20 draws if
20 x (cnn + hmm step time) fit in 25 GPU-session hours. The projection was
21.96 h, so N = 20, committed on 2026-10-28 before any v3 metric existed.
With N = 20 the smallest possible p is 1/21 = 0.0476. More draws would not
change the reading here: the observed macro-F1 (0.371) was below all 20
permuted values (null mean 0.467), so p = 1.0000.
Source: EVALUATION_PROTOCOL.md section 9 (D2 addendum);
DOCS/plans/v3_result.txt.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
19. Why is the per-exercise accuracy lower than the family models?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Mainly data. A per-exercise model trains on one image per participant, about
217 images per fold, while a v1 family model trains on every crop of that
family for the same participants (4, 15 or 5 crops each). Early stopping,
HMM selection and Platt calibration all lean on the same 39
inner-validation participants per fold, which makes those choices noisy.
The fused CNN-HMM over all 24 exercises had macro-F1 0.371 [0.337, 0.410],
lower than v1's fused CNN-HMM by -0.063 [-0.118, -0.005] on the same
participants; its accuracy of 0.534 with a balanced accuracy of 0.494 shows
it predicted mostly one class. The fused CNN head did not differ from its v1
counterparts: every family difference had a CI that included zero.
Source: DOCS/plans/v3_result.txt (sections 2, 3 and 6).
